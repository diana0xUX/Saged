From stone to pigment to clay

Our first cultural research project. Painter Katya explores how natural stones become pigments for painting on ceramic.

The project

What we're researching and why

Before paint came in tubes, colour came from the ground. Ochres, umbers, and blue and green minerals were crushed, washed, and ground by hand. The painter's knowledge was inseparable from the material.

In the Materials Lab, Katya is reviving that knowledge for ceramics. She makes pigments from natural stones and tests them on clay slabs — fired and unfired — to understand how colour changes through heat.

The focus is the methodology: a repeatable, documented way to make pigment from stone, written down step by step so the knowledge can be shared. Stones from around Valencia join the palette wherever they can.

Methodology

The process, step by step

Every stone goes through the same five steps, so results can be compared and shared. The method is the product — not just the pigments it produces.

  1. 1

    Select

    Choose and identify the stone — name, source, mineral family.

  2. 2

    Grind

    Crush and grind by hand into a fine powder.

  3. 3

    Refine

    Wash and settle out the finest particle pigment.

  4. 4

    Paint

    Bind the pigment and paint test slabs, fired and unfired.

  5. 5

    Fire

    Fire some slabs and record exactly how the colour changes.

October 2026 kickoff methodology

Season 1 begins

Materials Lab is Saged Club's first cultural research project, and it starts this month. Painter Katya is spending the next year learning how natural stones become pigments for painting on clay.

The focus isn't just making pigments — it's writing the method down. A repeatable, step-by-step process that anyone can follow, adapt, and build on. At the end of Season 1 we'll share it at a public open session and a sample show here at the studio.

The approach: every stone goes through the same five steps — select, grind, refine, paint, fire. The method stays constant. The variable is the stone. That's how you learn what the stone is actually doing.
