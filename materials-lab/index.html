Pigment Lab · A research project of Asociación Cultural Saged Season 1 · Nov 2026 – Nov 2027

Colour from Spanish earth

A year-long artistic research project by Ekaterina Shárova. The project assembles a collection of natural pigments obtained exclusively from Spanish minerals, earths and plants, and documents their behaviour on paper and in ceramics.

The project

About the project

Before industrial paint, colour was obtained directly from the land. Ochres, umbers, green and blue minerals, roots, leaves and flowers were collected, ground and washed by hand, and the painter's knowledge was inseparable from the material.

Pigment Lab aims to assemble as complete a collection as possible of natural pigments sourced solely from Spanish territory, both mineral and botanical. Following training in traditional pigment extraction, the artist collects material in the Valencian Community, Granada and Andalusia, and Galicia.

The research addresses two media: paper and ceramics. Pigments unsuitable for firing remain valuable for work on paper. The project seeks the widest possible palette and a method that can be transmitted to other artists, enabling them to prepare their own colours rather than rely on synthetic or laboratory-made products.

Research question
"Which pigments can be made from the minerals, earths and plants of Spain without synthetic additives — and how do they behave on paper and in ceramics?"
1

What the land holds

Which minerals, earths and plants in each region can give colour.

2

On paper

How each pigment works on paper, including how well it holds up to light.

3

In ceramics

Which minerals work on clay through firing, beyond the familiar iron oxide.

Expected outcomes

The number of pigments cannot be known in advance; establishing it is part of the research. The outcomes are therefore defined qualitatively:

  • — A map of sources, including locations and materials that did not yield a usable pigment
  • — A pigment library with brushstroke samples and test pieces on paper and clay
  • — A documented, reproducible method
  • — A record of unexpected textures and "defects", treated as findings in their own right
  • — Two public events: an open laboratory presentation (May 2027) and a final exhibition (November 2027)
Methodology

The process, step by step

Each material follows the same sequence of steps so that results are comparable. The base method draws on the artist's training at The King's Foundation School of Traditional Arts (November 2026).

  1. 1

    Source

    Collection of the mineral, earth or plant, with its place of origin recorded.

  2. 2

    Extract

    Grinding and washing of minerals; extraction of colour from plants.

  3. 3

    Refine

    Settling and drying of the finest pigment fraction.

  4. 4

    Paper tests

    Preparation of paint; brushstroke and lightfastness tests on paper.

  5. 5

    Ceramic tests

    Painted test pieces, fired to record changes in colour.

  6. 6

    Record

    Entry into the map and library, including unsuccessful results.

From the process

Safety and care. Wet grinding is used throughout to minimise dust. Toxic or suspect minerals are excluded from the palette. All painted ceramic pieces produced in the lab are decorative only and not suitable for food use.

November 2026 kickoff course

Opening of Season 1

Pigment Lab is the first research project of Asociación Cultural Saged. Over the coming year, Ekaterina Shárova will collect colour from Spanish minerals, earths and plants, and study the behaviour of each pigment on paper and in ceramics.

The project opens with training. In November the artist studies pigment extraction from minerals and plants at The King's Foundation School of Traditional Arts. This establishes the base method applied to every subsequent material.

Fieldwork follows: the Valencian Community in winter, Granada and Andalusia in spring, and Galicia in summer. All findings, including unsuccessful ones, are recorded on the map of sources.

Further updates will be published here as the work progresses.

Public events

Two events

The first presents the method and its applications; the second presents the artistic outcome.

May 2027 · date to be announced

Inside the Pigment Lab

A presentation of the working laboratory: pigments, brushstroke samples, test pieces on clay and paper, and illustrations demonstrating their application. Intended for ceramicists and artists interested in preparing their own paints.

Depending on interest, the presentation may be followed by a training programme for artists.

Season 1 finale

Colour from Spanish earth

The closing exhibition focuses on the artistic result rather than the method: the land, the region, its traditions and the symbolism of colour, presented through ceramic and paper works made with the pigments gathered during the year.
