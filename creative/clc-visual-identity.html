Colour combinations — what works
White on Purple
Primary text combination. Used for all body copy and headlines on purple backgrounds. High contrast, high legibility.
White on Orange
Button labels. CTA text. Always white on orange — never purple or dark text on orange buttons.
Purple on Orange
Display headlines on the orange footer strip. The only instance of purple text — and only in the Boogaloo display font.
Purple on White
Links, nav items, secondary actions in content areas. Also used for logo/wordmark on white backgrounds.
Ink on White
Body text in content sections. The default for all long-form reading.
Orange on Purple
Accent headlines, price callouts, emphasis text on purple backgrounds. Used sparingly.
Colour rule

Purple is the world. Orange is the door. Every page lives in purple. Every moment of action (button, CTA strip, confirmation state) is orange. The two colours never compete — orange only appears when something needs to happen. This binary creates instant clarity: if it's orange, click it.

Chapter 05
Design Principles
The rules behind every visual decision — observed, not stated.
01
Purple is total, orange is singular
Purple owns every surface. Orange appears exactly once per view — the primary action. This is not a guideline, it is structural: if you put two orange elements on a page, neither works. The system depends on orange being the rarest colour in any given frame.
02
Chalk for moments, Inter for everything else
Boogaloo (or the chalk equivalent) is not a display font for frequent use — it is a personality signal deployed at key moments: the hero, the footer, the wordmark. Inter carries 90% of all reading. The playfulness comes from the rare display moments, not from making body copy quirky.
03
One question at a time
The onboarding flow puts a single question on a full purple page. No sidebar, no progress summary, no previews. This is a deliberate UX choice: one question feels conversational, not like filling in a form. It reduces perceived effort and signals that the brand values your attention, not your data.
04
Understate the scale, lead with the moment
100+ cities. 1000s of members. This is never the headline. The headline is always two people, one lunch, one city. Scale is proof, not pitch. Visually this means small group photos, intimate card layouts, first-person testimonials — never dashboards, maps, or aggregate statistics as hero content.
05
Real people, raw moments
Photography is mid-conversation, natural light, small groups. The camera catches something already happening — it never poses. This is non-negotiable: the brand's authenticity claim collapses the moment a stock photo appears. Photos must feel like someone's phone, not a brand shoot.
06
The brand is a person, not a platform
Klaus writes in first person on the about page and tells you to DM him. This founder-voice carries through all copy: "I" not "we," direct address, casual register. Design should match: human-scaled type, generous white space, no feature lists or matrix tables. It's a conversation, not a product catalogue.
Chapter 06
Brand in Use
How to apply CLC's visual language when collaborating as a host venue.
What CLC expects from a host's venue page

Visual requirements

→Real photos of the space with people in it — not empty room shots
→Natural light preferred — warm and approachable
→Show the community — group shots, hands at work, conversation
→The food — even informal, even simple — deserves a photo

Copy requirements

→First person, present tense: "We have..." not "The studio features..."
→People before features: who comes here, not what equipment you have
→3–5 sentences max — their format is tight
→End on a feeling, not a fact: "warm," "unhurried," "you stay longer than planned"
Saged × CLC — venue description (approved copy)
How Saged describes itself in CLC's language

"We have an international creative community studio in Valencia's old city — a ceramic workshop and coworking space where makers, artists, and curious locals come to work with clay, share a table, and slow down. The studio has kilns, a hand-building room, and open coworking desks. People here are a mix of expats, locals, and creatives who found each other through ceramics. The atmosphere is relaxed and hands-on — think mismatched mugs, clay dust, and good conversation. Perfect for a lunch where people actually talk."

Food & experience — the vegan buffet frame
"We'll ask one of our community members to prepare a vegan buffet — we have people in our circle who cook and love an excuse to feed a creative crowd."
This matches CLC's ethos perfectly: community-sourced, casual, personal. Not catered, not corporate.
Hands-on activity offer
"We'd love to make it a hands-on experience — guests could try a quick pinch pot or leave with a small clay piece they made during the lunch."
This is a differentiator. CLC's best venue pages have a memorable hook — this is Saged's. Keep it in every communication with them.
