Brand essence
One sentence

Anti-corporate networking dressed as lunch — a global community that turns the awkward business card ritual into a genuine meal between two strangers who might become collaborators, clients, or friends.

The core tension they resolve

Freelance creatives are professionally isolated. They want to grow their network but hate networking events — they feel transactional, stiff, forgettable. Creative Lunch Club removes the event format entirely and replaces it with something everyone already does: eat lunch. The food is the excuse. The connection is the product.

Brand personality

Five words that describe how the brand behaves — not what it sells.

Casually warm Founder-voiced Unpretentious Quietly global Genuinely human

The brand never shows off its scale (100+ cities). It leads with intimacy — two people having lunch. The global reach is the proof point, not the pitch.

How they use colour

Purple is the identity — saturated, confident, unexpected for a food/social brand. It signals creativity without trying to look like a design agency. Orange is strictly the action colour: every button, every CTA strip, every moment where something needs to happen is orange. White creates breathing room inside the purple world. The combination reads as playful and warm, not corporate-tech.

Type rules

Display

All-caps, hand-drawn style. Used for hero moments only — footer CTA, section headers, event titles. Never body copy.

Headlines

Clean sans-serif, bold or semibold. Short. Benefit-first. "Meet other creatives for lunch." Not "Our platform connects creatives."

Body

Regular weight, comfortable size. Conversational sentences. No bullet-point abuse. Reads like a person talking, not a brand deck.

Voice principles

Personal, not corporate. Klaus writes in first person on the about page. The brand is a person, not an institution. Use "I" and "you."

Problem-first. Name the thing people hate ("networking events are boring") before offering the fix. They feel seen first.

Understate the scale. 100+ cities is buried. The pitch is always two people at lunch, not a global platform.

Let members speak. The reviews page is the brand's strongest asset. Real quotes, real names, real outcomes. No paraphrasing.

Casual confidence. "Et voilà." "Easy as that." Short, complete sentences. No hedging. No "we believe" or "we strive to."

Outcome-specific. "Make friends." "Find clients." "Get inspired." Not "grow your network" — too vague and too LinkedIn.

Target audience
Primary — the freelancer
Works alone, from home or a café. Misses the collision of office life — the hallway conversation, the lunch table. Professionally capable but socially isolated. Wants peers, not mentors.
Secondary — the agency creative
Works at a small creative studio. Wants perspective from outside their bubble. Looking for collaborators, referrals, and fresh ideas from adjacent fields. Tired of LinkedIn.
Tertiary — the creative switcher
Just moved city, just went freelance, just changed field. Needs to rebuild their network from scratch. CLC is the fastest way to meet people without the awkwardness of cold outreach.

What they never say to their audience

"Accelerate your career." "Unlock your potential." "Level up." "Scale your business." — These are the phrases of hustle culture and self-help marketing. CLC's audience is exhausted by that register. The brand speaks to the human need (connection, belonging, conversation) and lets professional outcomes follow quietly.

What these quotes tell you about brand perception

Members don't talk about features (matching, cities, meetups). They talk about feelings: finding their people, belonging, ease. The brand has successfully shifted its value proposition from "networking tool" to "community." That's the hardest thing to do in B2C — and they've done it through consistent voice and format, not advertising.

Positioning statement (reverse-engineered)

For freelance and independent creative professionals who are professionally capable but personally isolated, Creative Lunch Club is the community that turns lunch into belonging — because unlike LinkedIn, networking apps, or industry events, CLC removes the professional performance entirely and replaces it with the most natural human ritual: sharing a meal.
