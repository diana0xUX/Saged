## How risks changed the number

The original business case put the working valuation at €30-45K. The asset audit and lease analysis sharpened it downward:

### Lease findings that pushed valuation down
- **3-year term ending 2029-02-01** with no auto-renewal — material discount on intangibles
- **Clause 16 anti-assignment** — makes SL formation more friction-heavy; can't bake landlord cooperation into the price
- **Clause 3 license acknowledgment** — Diana signed acknowledging no license; this is a documented going-concern risk
- **Clause 11 maintenance burden** — ongoing operating cost is higher than gross rent implies
- **€6,300 trapped deposit** — not lost, but not free working capital either

### Audit findings that pushed valuation down
- **Tangible assets only €4-6K** — much less than typical ceramic studios (€15-30K)
- **No kiln ventilation, no clay trap, no safety gear** — €1,700-4,500 to bring to compliance

### Findings that pushed valuation up (offsetting)
- **Ciutat Vella location moat** — still in place, still a real advantage
- **RU/UA diaspora niche** — confirmed in competitive analysis, no other operator competes there
- **Real product-market signal** — 11 attendees at the recent masterclass, 6 booked Thursday cohorts, repeat customers in May
- **Meta Pixel + Cal.com + bilingual site operating** — operational maturity meaningful for a partner who's not technical
- **Tenant unilateral exit right after year 1** — optionality (Diana can walk away if business fails, vs being trapped in a 5-year lease)
- **Camera as studio asset (€1,500)** — net new addition to balance sheet

**Net effect: roughly a €5-10K downward adjustment** vs the original estimate.

## Recommended deal structure (revised)

The husband partnership conversation has three variables to set: **pre-money valuation**, **his contribution**, and **equity split**. They are linked.

### Scenario 1 — Husband €15K, 33% equity (clean investor frame)

| | |
|---|---|
| Pre-money valuation | €30,000 |
| Husband contribution (cash) | €15,000 |
| Post-money valuation | €45,000 |
| Husband equity | 33% |
| Diana equity | 67% |

**When this fits**: husband sees himself primarily as a capital partner, not a daily operator. Diana stays the operating face of the business; husband contributes capital + occasional strategic input.

**Note**: post-money €45K is higher than fair-value €30K because his capital is value-creating (it closes compliance gap, funds the license, removes the closure risk).

### Scenario 2 — Husband €15K + time, 40-45% equity

| | |
|---|---|
| Pre-money valuation | €30,000 |
| Husband contribution (cash + ~20 hrs/wk over 12 months) | €25,000 effective |
| Post-money valuation | €55,000 effective |
| Husband equity | 40-45% |
| Diana equity | 55-60% |

**When this fits**: husband works in the business 15-25 hrs/wk on partnerships, B2B, finance, admin (the gaps Diana doesn't fill). Time is valued at ~€20-25/hr (below-market, reflecting co-owner status).

**Diana keeps the edge** because she carries the personal lease liability + already-built business.

### Scenario 3 — True 50/50 (works only if husband puts in more)

| | |
|---|---|
| Pre-money valuation | €30,000 |
| Husband contribution (cash) | €30,000 |
| Post-money valuation | €60,000 |
| Husband equity | 50% |
| Diana equity | 50% |

**When this fits**: husband brings €30K+ in cash AND commits full-time. This matches Diana's contribution (€30K business value + ongoing time + personal lease liability).

**At less than €25K cash for 50/50**, Diana is structurally undervalued — even between spouses, this creates resentment down the line if the marriage drifts.

### My recommendation

**Scenario 2 — husband €15K cash + 20 hrs/wk for first year + 40% equity, Diana 60%.**

Why:
- €15K is the realistic capital injection number (Diana mentioned this range before)
- The 60/40 in Diana's favor honors that she built the business, signed the lease personally, and carries the going-concern risk
- 40% is meaningful enough that husband feels co-owner, not employee
- Leaves room to renegotiate to 50/50 later (e.g., when SL is established, license is obtained, and the 2029 lease is renewed into the SL's name — that's a natural "phase 2 equity check-in" moment)

## What this means for the conversation with him

**The valuation number to walk into the conversation with: €30,000 pre-money.**

If he pushes back ("I think the business is worth €50K because the website + brand alone…"), you can show him:
- Asset audit: tangibles €4-6K
- Lease cliff in 32 months
- Documented license gap
- No assignability without landlord OK

These are not bargaining-down moves — they're real factors that a third-party valuer would price in.

If he pushes back the other way ("It's worth less than €30K because there's no profit yet"):
- Real customer base + repeat bookings
- Working ad pipeline (Meta Pixel + audiences trained)
- Bilingual site + brand identity worth €7-12K to recreate
- Operational tooling (Cal.com, Stripe, CAPI configured)
- Korytsia relationship — replacement cost €5-10K minimum

**The €30K number sits inside both perspectives' fairness range.**
