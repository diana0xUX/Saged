## Critical clauses for the partnership decision

### 🔴 Clause 16 — Cesión y subarriendo (anti-assignment)

> "Se prohíbe expresamente a la arrendataria la cesión, subarriendo o traspaso del inmueble arrendado, total o parcialmente, salvo autorización previa, expresa y escrita en cada ocasión por la propiedad."

**Translation**: Diana cannot assign, sublease, or transfer the lease — partially or fully — without **written consent from the landlord each time**.

**Why this matters**: The standard right to assign a commercial lease (LAU article 32, which normally lets a tenant assign with a 20% rent bump) has been waived.

**Implications for forming a Saged S.L. with husband:**

1. Moving the lease from "Diana personally" → "Saged S.L." (where Diana + husband are shareholders) **requires landlord's written approval**. Landlord can refuse, or demand a fee/rent bump.
2. The simplest workaround is **not to move the lease at all**: keep it in Diana's name, have the SL pay her rent as a business expense (effectively Diana sub-leases to the SL informally). This works financially but the **legal tenant remains Diana personally**, so:
   - All landlord-tenant liability stays with her
   - If Diana ever leaves the marriage/partnership, the lease leaves with her
   - The SL has no real estate asset on its books
3. Cleanest path is **approach landlord** with a request to assign the lease to the new SL. Expect: 5-10% rent bump or one-off "consentimiento" fee. Landlord is sophisticated (used Docusign + a juristas firm) — he'll know his leverage.

### 🟡 Clause 4 — Duración del contrato (3-year cliff)

> "El plazo de duración del presente contrato, libremente estipulada por las partes contratantes es de TRES AÑOS, a contar desde 1-02-2026."
> 
> "El arrendador comunica en este acto que, al término del vencimiento del plazo estipulado, este contrato quedará extinguido sin que sea aplicable prorroga alguna."

**Translation**: 3-year term; at end of term, contract simply ends — no auto-renewal. Renewal requires new negotiation.

**Why this matters**:
- The studio has **secure occupancy until 2029-02-01** — about 32 months from now.
- After that: renegotiation is the only path. Landlord has explicitly stated his intent to "recover possession" at end of term.
- Spanish commercial leases sometimes auto-renew via the LAU defaults — **this contract has been written to override that**.
- Diana has **unilateral exit right after year 1** (1 month notice, no penalty) — useful if the business doesn't work out.

**Implications for capex planning**:
- Closing the compliance gaps (kiln vent, clay trap, signage) makes financial sense — the cost amortizes over 2.5 years of guaranteed occupancy.
- Bigger capex (proper ventilation system, fixed bench installations) only makes sense if you're confident of a 2029+ renewal.
- **Chain scaling plans (Scenarios C/D in the business case) need to plan around this lease cliff or budget for relocation in 2029.**

### 🔴 Clause 3 — Destino (license acknowledgment)

> "Manifiesta el arrendatario, que el local objeto de este contrato será destinado para su uso personal como estudio de cerámica, siendo conocedora la parte ARRENDATARIA, que actualmente no tiene licencia."

**Translation**: Diana acknowledges in writing that **she does not currently have the opening license** for ceramic-studio use.

**Why this matters**:
- Landlord has shielded himself with documented evidence Diana operates unlicensed.
- Failure to obtain the license **does not invalidate the rent obligation** (per Clause 9): "La validez del presente contrato de arrendamiento no se verá afectada si el arrendatario no obtiene alguna de las licencias necesarias."
- Tenant is solely liable for any city fines or closures.
- **Combined with the missing kiln ventilation + sink/clay trap (from the asset audit)**: this is an actual operating risk, not just a paper one. A neighbor complaint → city inspection → forced closure is on the table.

**Action for the partnership**:
- First call of husband's capital should fund **licencia de apertura + actividad clasificada** application via gestor (€500-2,000) AND the compliance gear that the license will require (kiln vent + clay trap + safety + signage = ~€1,200-2,500).
- Total compliance + license: ~€1,700-4,500. Treat as **non-negotiable first spend** before any marketing or growth investment.

### 🟡 Clause 11 — Obras y gastos de conservación

> "Con renuncia expresa a lo dispuesto en el artículo 30, en relación con el artículo 21 de la LAU, el Arrendatario vendrá obligado a mantener en buen estado de uso y conservación el local arrendado..."

**Translation**: Diana has waived the landlord's standard maintenance duty (LAU art. 30/21). **All repairs and maintenance are her responsibility**, including electrical, plumbing, doors, windows, glass.

**Implications**:
- Any major repair (e.g., the kiln damages the floor, the slab roller breaks a beam, the wood floor warps from water) = Diana pays.
- The €6,300 deposit is at risk if the studio is returned in non-original condition.
- Insurance (Clause 10) covers fire/flood/civil liability but **not maintenance-related claims**.
- Practical mitigation: budget €100-200/mo into a maintenance reserve. Document the studio's current condition with photos in case of dispute at lease-end.

### 🟢 Clause 12 — Obras del arrendatario (improvements)

- 3 months from signing (1 Feb → 1 May 2026) to do startup works.
- After that: written permission required for non-decorative work.
- "Obras menores de decoración y adaptación" always allowed without permission.
- **At lease end: all improvements stay with the property** (no compensation, per Clause 18).

**Implications**:
- The 3-month startup window already expired. Any structural work going forward (e.g., installing a kiln ventilation hood that requires roof penetration) **needs landlord's written permission**.
- This is a real obstacle to installing the missing kiln vent. Need to approach landlord with a "proyecto de obra" before proceeding.
- All improvement value (decoration, signage, fixtures) is sunk cost — recovered only via continued occupancy.

### 🟡 Clause 17 — Renuncia al derecho de adquisición preferente

Diana has waived the right of first refusal (LAU article 31) if the landlord sells the property.

**Implication**: A new owner could refuse to renew. Strategic risk for any plan that needs >3 years.

### 🟡 Clause 23 — Force majeure waiver

> "La parte arrendataria renuncia expresamente a solicitar carencias, moratorias, aplazamientos, reducciones de renta o renegociaciones contractuales por cualquiera de las circunstancias anteriormente descritas..."

Diana has waived the right to request rent reductions in a crisis (war, pandemic, economic crisis, lockdown, etc.).

**Counter-balance**: Diana can still **unilaterally terminate** the contract in such a crisis without penalty — but cannot reduce rent.

**Implication**: in a downturn, the only lever is to leave entirely. No "pay 50% until things recover" option.

### 🟢 Clause 22 — Confidentiality

Both parties must keep contract terms confidential indefinitely. **Don't post contract clauses publicly** (e.g., on the public Saged.club site or social media). Sharing with gestor, husband, lawyer = fine (they're necessary).

### Recommended partnership structure given lease constraints

The earlier business case recommended forming a new SL. With the lease analysis, the **clean path** is:

1. **Keep lease in Diana's personal name** (no landlord approval needed)
2. **Form the SL** ("Saged Studio S.L." or similar) with Diana + husband as shareholders
3. **SL leases the space from Diana** via a simple internal sub-lease at the same €1,173/mo — this passes the rent through, lets the SL deduct it as a business expense, and keeps the landlord relationship simple

5. **Re-negotiate the lease into the SL's name only when approaching 2029 renewal** — combines the two negotiations and is the natural moment to restructure

This avoids triggering Clause 16 today while still capturing the full SL benefit. **Gestor will know the right form** — confirm at the consult.
